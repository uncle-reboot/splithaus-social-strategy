3. Narrator voice guide for the House

Before: The source specifies a warm, dry older male House narrator; Bill was used in a draft, but fit and pace are unverified.

tone

Warm, dry, patient, faintly amused; affection through specific observations rather than declarations.

performance

One House voice briefly quotes character dialogue. No separate character narration or lip-sync. Start at 60–70 audible words; shorter is acceptable. Average 6–9 words per sentence, maximum 14. Absolute ceiling 85, subject to an unhurried timed read. Leave at least three seconds without speech.

endings

Spoken story ending; optional participation question in the post caption only. Total runtime includes pauses and the final show identifier.

knowledge

Witnessed events in and around the cabin, and information explicitly spoken or shown there. No private thoughts, distant unseen reactions, future knowledge, or inferred product functionality.

sample lines
  • I've kept that pencil mark since nineteen sixty-six.
  • I remember the sandwich. Dave remembers a tradition.
  • I heard the date. Then nobody said much.
  • Dave carried her bag. For once, without instructions.
  • Linda brought refreshments for the negotiations.
avoid
  • Generic sentiment: making memories, cherished moments, forever.
  • Marketing: seamless, solution, sign up, product recommendations.
  • Death, illness, legal, financing, and ownership vocabulary in the scripts.
  • Jokes about age, frailty, or technology incompetence.
voice validation

Bill remains a candidate. Check warmth, pacing, pronunciation of SplitHaus and Halloran, and unwanted sadness. Prefer cabin without treating lake house as a factual error.

Risk: Word counts do not establish runtime or an unhurried delivery; caption-only questions differ from Showrunner and Voice Director preferences.
If rejected: Shorten scripts or revise holds after a timed read; do not accelerate narration or exceed 35 seconds to preserve extra dialogue.
